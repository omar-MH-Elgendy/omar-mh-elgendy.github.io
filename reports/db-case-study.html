The problem

Three seller accounts on three marketplaces produced three kinds of numbers that never matched: what the marketplace says it paid, what the sales report says was sold, and what I owe the supplier. A return could be counted as lost revenue in one file and ignored in another, and a failed delivery still carries the supplier's handling fee. Nobody could answer "what did we really earn on this product?" with confidence.

Architecture: data moves one way

Raw files and API pulls land untouched, get unified into core tables, feed the ledgers, and only then reach reports. A page or script may read the ledger or reporting layers, never the raw layer.

Five ledgers, one rule

I wrote the business down as five ledgers and made one rule: every figure in every report must be derived from these ledgers only, through shared views. No dashboard may calculate "sales" or "returns" its own way.

LedgerWhat it recordsKey decision
SalesOrders that were actually shippedCancelled-before-shipping orders appear nowhere. Sales date = order date, even if settled later.
PurchasesGoods cost + the supplier's handling fee, per shipped orderThe handling fee is charged on every shipped order, including returns and failed deliveries, and is never refunded.
ReturnsReturns, failed deliveries, item condition, claimsOnly an item proven to be returned in good condition (photo or video evidence) credits the supplier balance.
Marketplace moneyFees, settlements, payouts to the bankEvery amount is flagged ACTUAL (settled) or ESTIMATED, so estimates never mix with real money.
TransfersPayments to the supplier, the owner's share, marketplace compensationCompensation reduces a loss; it is never counted as sales.

Two questions the database answers

Scope of the figures. This page covers Aug–Nov 2026 from reporting.rpt_order_profit. The two insight reports use the same view and the same export (Apr–Nov 2026), so totals agree; November is month to date. Margins here divide by all order amounts, including lines whose purchase cost is not known yet, so they are slightly conservative.

Both queries run against the reporting layer (reporting.rpt_order_profit). The results below are the real output from the live database; product codes are masked.

1. Is each platform really profitable, by month?

Net profit comes from the marketplace's actual settlement minus goods cost and handling fee, not from list prices.

SELECT to_char(period_month,'YYYY-MM') AS month, platform,
       count(*) AS orders,
       round(sum(order_amount)) AS revenue,
       round(sum(net_order_profit)) AS net_profit,
       round(100*sum(net_order_profit)/NULLIF(sum(order_amount),0),1) AS margin_pct
FROM   reporting.rpt_order_profit
WHERE  sales_classification IN ('SALE','RETURN','PARTIAL_RETURN')
GROUP  BY 1,2 ORDER BY 1 DESC, 2;

2. Which products lose money, and how much of it is returns?

The decision this drives: stop reordering, raise the price, or fix the listing. (Aug–Nov 2026, products with at least 5 orders.)

SELECT product_ic, count(*) AS orders,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE sales_classification IN ('RETURN','PARTIAL_RETURN')) AS returned,
       round(100.0*count(*) FILTER (WHERE sales_classification IN ('RETURN','PARTIAL_RETURN'))/count(*),1) AS return_rate_pct,
       round(sum(net_order_profit)) AS net_profit
FROM   reporting.rpt_order_profit
WHERE  period_month >= date '2026-08-01'
GROUP  BY product_ic
HAVING count(*) >= 5 AND sum(net_order_profit) < 0
ORDER  BY net_profit;

Order mix in the same layer (Aug–Nov 2026, 811 lines): 744 sales, 66 returns, 1 partial return; 80 lines are still incomplete or estimated and flagged by data_quality_status rather than silently counted.

Rules I had to decide (and write down)

SituationDecisionWhy it matters
Order shipped but the marketplace has not settled it yetExcluded from total sales and shown as "pending" with an age flag (red after 21 days)Prevents counting revenue that may still reverse.
Failed deliveryRemoved from net sales; goods cost credited back only if the item returned in good condition; handling fee staysWithout this, failed deliveries looked cost-free.
Returned with no proof of conditionNo supplier deduction; flagged "still being verified", not "not returned"Avoids disputes over unproven claims.
Marketplace compensation for a lost or damaged itemRecorded on the date received, as a reduction of loss, never as salesKeeps sales and profit honest.
Closed accounting periodLocked; corrections are posted as entries in the open periodPast reports never change silently.
Estimated vs actual marketplace amountsEvery amount carries an ACTUAL/ESTIMATED flagReports never mix guesses with money that really arrived.

What I would improve next

The database has 109 declared foreign keys, and the core ledger path is fully constrained (see the diagram). Many operational tables (job queues, logs, worker state) are intentionally unconstrained, but some finance tables still relate to each other only by shared keys such as account and order number. Next step: add real constraints there, and a tenant_id column on core, finance, ops and pricing tables so the same system can serve other clients.
